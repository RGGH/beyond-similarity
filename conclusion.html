Scene 08 — Conclusion

Two jobs, done by two different parts of the system.

A short recap, tying retrieval and ranking back together.

OBSERVATION

"Vector search gets us relevant candidates. Ranking logic determines which candidates should appear first."

01Summary

Everything in this guide sits on that one distinction. Semantic similarity is necessary — without it, there's no candidate set worth ranking. But it was never sufficient on its own, and treating it as the whole answer is how a search system quietly stops matching what a business actually needs.

Formula Query doesn't replace the vector search — it sits directly on top of it, close enough to reuse the similarity score itself, and just as easy to change as a weight on a slider.

03Where to go from here

Try the demo

Run Boosted Search against your own collection — swap in different payload fields and see which ones actually move the ranking.

Read up on decay shapes

Linear, exponential and Gaussian each suit a different kind of "distance from ideal" — pick based on how forgiving the signal should be.

Desmos is your friend, or if it's not already it's well worth trying out

Watch for the LTR moment

When hand-tuned weights stop feeling adequate and interaction data is piling up, that's the signal to look at Learn-to-Rank.

Keep the formula transparent

Even after adopting more advanced ranking, a simple Formula Query fallback is worth keeping around — for debugging, and for new items with no history yet.
