Scene 02 — Problem & Concept

Relevant isn't the same question as "what should I recommend?"

Vector similarity answers one question well.

A recommendation surface has to answer a different, messier one.

01What vector similarity gives you

A dense embedding puts a query and a catalog* of products in the same space, and nearest-neighbour search returns the items closest to that query. That's semantic relevance — how well an item matches the meaning of what was asked for.

(correct spelling = "catalogue"😉)

It's a genuinely hard problem, and modern vector databases (Qdrant) solve it well. But "close in embedding space" quietly assumes that closeness is the only thing that matters for what gets shown first — and in a real storefront, it isn't.

02What a recommendation actually needs

Two items can sit at nearly identical distance from a query and still deserve very different treatment:

Item A

Semantically on point, but it's an out-of-stock style from three seasons ago that nobody has bought recently.

Item B

Slightly less "on the nose" semantically, but it's a current bestseller with strong recent purchase activity.

A storefront usually wants to surface Item B first. Similarity alone can't tell them apart — it has no concept of stock, trend, or business priority. That gap is where additional signals come in: rating, popularity, recency, margin, customer preference, whatever the business actually cares about.

03Think of ranking as a scoring function

The reframe that makes this tractable: stop thinking of "search" and "ranking" as one step. Retrieval finds the candidates. Ranking is a separate function that takes each candidate and produces a number — a score — used purely to order the results.

That score can be built from anything measurable: the semantic similarity is one input among several, not the whole answer.

semantic similarity + business signals = ranking score

The next scene looks at the dataset this score will be built from — and specifically, which fields belong to the vector side of the problem and which belong to the business-logic side.
